Temporary email: frequently asked questions

Short, honest answers about how HourglassPost and disposable inboxes in general work.

What is a temporary email address?

It's a working email address you can use for a short time and then forget. HourglassPost creates one for you as soon as the page opens. Mail sent to it shows up in the inbox on the page, and after about an hour the messages are deleted. It's handy whenever a website wants an email address but you don't want to hand over your real one.

Is HourglassPost free? Do I need to sign up?

Yes, it's free, and there's no sign-up, password or app to install. Just open the page and copy the address.

How long do my address and its messages last?

Messages are kept for about an hour, and the page shows roughly how many minutes your address has left. When the time runs out, click New address to get a fresh one. Don't count on anything staying there longer, and copy any code or link you need straight away.

Can other people read my temporary inbox?

Yes, possibly. Temporary inboxes are public: there's no password, so anyone who knows or guesses the address can read the mail sent to it. HourglassPost picks long random addresses to make guessing unlikely, but you should still treat everything that arrives as readable by others.

Can I send or reply to email from HourglassPost?

No. HourglassPost only receives mail. There's no way to send, reply or forward, which also keeps the service from being used to send spam.

Why hasn't my email arrived?
  • Give it a minute. Some senders are slow, and the inbox checks for new mail about every 10 seconds (you can also press the refresh button).
  • Make sure you pasted the full address, including the part after the @.
  • Check that the address hasn't expired. If it has, get a new one and request the email again.
  • Some websites refuse disposable addresses or quietly drop mail to them. If that happens, that site simply won't work with a temporary inbox.

Should I use a temporary email for my bank, work or social media accounts?

No. If you sign up for an important account with a temporary address, you won't be able to reset your password or receive security alerts once the address is gone, and you could lose the account for good. Because the inbox is public, someone else could also read your reset links. Use your real email for anything you want to keep.

Is it safe to open messages here?

HourglassPost shows each email in a locked-down frame: scripts, forms and remote images (including tracking pixels) are blocked, and there's a plain-text view too. That removes the most common risks, but normal email sense still applies: don't download unexpected files, and be careful with links from senders you don't know.

Can I get the same address back later?

While the tab stays open (or you reload it), HourglassPost keeps your current address. Once you close the tab, it's forgotten, and there's no account to recover it with. Plan on each address being used once.

Can I choose a different domain?

Yes. Use the Domain menu next to your address to pick another Guerrilla Mail domain, such as @sharklasers.com, @grr.la or @spam4.me. It's still the same inbox: only the part after the @ changes in the address you see and copy, and mail sent to any of these domains arrives in the same place. Switching can help when a website rejects one domain. Your choice is remembered until you close the tab.

Can I get a Gmail, Yahoo, Hotmail or Outlook address?

No. Only Google can issue @gmail.com addresses, only Yahoo can issue @yahoo.com ones, and only Microsoft can issue @hotmail.com and @outlook.com ones. None of them offer disposable addresses. Temp mail sites that claim to give you one usually abuse the dot and plus tricks (for example j.ohn.smith+123@gmail.com) on an account that belongs to someone else, so a stranger can read your mail and the account can vanish at any moment. HourglassPost uses its mail provider's own disposable domains instead.

Does HourglassPost use cookies or track me?

HourglassPost has no cookies, analytics, ads or trackers of its own. Your current session is stored only in your browser tab (sessionStorage), and your light/dark choice in localStorage. The mailbox itself is run by Guerrilla Mail, which receives your requests directly. See our privacy policy for the details.
