How HourglassPost works

What happens when you open the page, how your mail is fetched and shown safely, and the limits you should know about.

Using HourglassPost in three steps

  1. 1

    Open the page

    A random address such as k7m2xq9fta@… is created automatically. There's nothing to fill in.

  2. 2

    Copy and paste it

    Press Copy and paste the address into the sign-up form, download page or newsletter box.

  3. 3

    Read the mail

    Keep the tab open. New messages appear on their own within about 10 seconds. Click one to read it.

What happens behind the scenes

HourglassPost is a small static website: just HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no server or database of its own. The mailboxes come from Guerrilla Mail, a long-running disposable email service with a free public API.

  • Creating an address. When the page opens, your browser asks Guerrilla Mail for a new mailbox with a random name. Guerrilla Mail returns the address and a session token.
  • Checking for mail. About every 10 seconds the page asks Guerrilla Mail whether anything new has arrived. Checking pauses while the tab is hidden and slows down if the service is busy, so we don't overload it.
  • Remembering your session. The token and address are kept in your tab's sessionStorage, so a reload keeps the same inbox. Closing the tab forgets them.
  • Expiry. Guerrilla Mail deletes messages after about an hour. The page shows a countdown and stops checking once the address expires.

Built-in safety

  • Emails open in a sandboxed frame: scripts, forms and pop-up tricks can't run.
  • Remote images are blocked, so senders can't use tracking pixels to see when you opened their message.
  • A plain-text view lets you read any message without its formatting.
  • No cookies are sent to the mail provider, and HourglassPost has no analytics or ads.

Limits to know about

  • Receive only: you can't send or reply.
  • Public: anyone with the address can read its mail. Never use it for anything private.
  • Short-lived: about one hour. Copy codes and links as soon as they arrive.
  • Not accepted everywhere: some websites block disposable email domains.

Tips for getting the most out of it

  • Use a temporary address for one-off downloads, free trials, store Wi-Fi, forum sign-ups you'll use once, or to test your own app's emails.
  • Use your real address for banking, shopping accounts you'll return to, work, school and anything with personal data.
  • If an email doesn't arrive after a couple of minutes, press refresh, then try a new address and request it again.
